About my website

Hello! This is my website, which I will use to talk about the three largest language families in the world. Based on the number of speakers, the largest language families are:

  1. Indo-European (3.2 billion speakers)
  2. Sino-Tibetan (1.3 billion speakers)
  3. Afro-Asiatic (668 million speakers)

Indo-European

Indo-European consists of most languages spoken in Europe as well as a large number of South Asian languages. They are all believed to have descended from an ancient unwritten language known as Proto-Indo-European. As you can probably guess, this family is massive, so I will provide a table that explains the different sub-families.

Indo-European Sub-Families
Sub-Family Descendant Script Examples of Members
Romance Vulgar Latin Latin Spanish, French, Portuguese, Italian, Romanian
Germanic Proto-Germanic Latin German, English, Dutch, Danish, Swedish
Celtic Proto-Celtic Latin Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Welsh, Breton
Slavic Proto-Slavic Cyrillic and Latin Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Serbo-Croatian, Bulgarian
Baltic Proto-Baltic Latin Latvian and Lithuanian
Indo-Aryan Proto-Indo-Iranian Many, including Devanagari and Bengali-Assamese Hindi, Urdu, Gujarati, Punjabi, Bengali
Iranic Proto-Indo-Iranian Perso-Arabic (Modified Arabic) Farsi, Dari, Pashto, Kurdish

For convenience, I left out a few families: Armenian, Hellenic (Greek), and Albanic (Albanian).These languages are independent, meaning they are currently the only surviving members of their language sub-family. These languages either have no extant relatives or do not have a known ancestor outside of Proto-Indo-European.

Sino-Tibetan

Sino-Tibetan consists entirely of languages concentrated in East Asia. It branches off into two sub-groups:

Sinitic, which consists of sub-groups that make up most languages spoken in China, including Mandarin, Hokkien (Southern Min), Shanghainese (Wu), and Cantonese (Yue). Here's an interesting fact: Chinese itself is not a language but instead is synonymous with the Sinetic family. What we refer to as Standard Chinese is actually based on Beijing dialects of Mandarin.

Tibeto-Burman,which consists of some languages spoken throughout Western China, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. This includes Tibetan, Dzongkha (or Bhutanese), Burmese, and Meitei (or Manipuri).
